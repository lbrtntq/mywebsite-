{# ── Q1 · Why ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
Q1

Why I built it

I play badminton. Open play is the best part of the week and the most chaotic: twenty people, three courts, and a host trying to remember who has waited longest.

Every group solves it with a whiteboard or a paddle stack, and every group argues about it. I wanted a phone to do the fair part, so the host can play too. PaQueueKa started as a fix for my own sessions. It now runs badminton and pickleball, with fees, clubs and rankings on top.

{# ── Q2 · One session ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
Q2

One session, start to finish

  1. Q2·1Set up the session

    Pick the sport, singles or doubles, how many courts and how scoring works. Then choose how the queue rotates.

  2. Q2·2Run the courts

    Players join from their own phones with a six-character code or a QR. Every court shows who is on, drawn on the court itself, with a running clock.

  3. Q2·3Plan who’s next

    Next Up projects the coming sets for every court, so nobody has to ask. Deploy a set to a free court in one tap, or add one by hand.

  4. Q2·4Score, then rotate

    Enter the score or just pick the winner. Saving it rotates the queue: players go to the back and the next match is ready.

  5. Q2·5Split the fees

    Court fee and shuttles in, shares out: by matches played, equally, per match or a flat rate. Mark people paid as they settle.

{# ── Q3 · Rotation modes ────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
Q3

Rotation modes

Every group plays differently, so Pro Host sessions get three ways to fill a court.

Just played Won, stays on Waiting

  1. Next on court

    PaQueueKa Rotation

    Balances skill, games played and who has partnered recently, so the same pairs don’t keep landing together.

  2. Next on court

    First in, first out

    Strict queue order. Whoever has waited longest plays next.

  3. Next on court

    Challenger’s Court

    Winners hold the court, and the host sets for how long.

Under the hood

One codebase for both stores, a Postgres backend, and a lot of tests, because a queue that’s wrong once loses the room.

Language
TypeScript
Backend
Supabase: Postgres, auth and edge functions
Live updates
Realtime Postgres changes, so every phone sees the same courts
State
Zustand
Tests
About 5,600 Jest tests in about 600 suites
Beyond sessions
Discover for finding clubs nearby; clubs, persistent rankings and a shared ledger with Pro Host
