Hey, I’m Albert.

I’m a data engineer from the Philippines. My day job is moving data from where it lands to where it’s useful, and keeping it moving.

Off the clock I shoot on my phone: streets, crowds crossing a square, towers stacked against the sky. I vlog a little and make the occasional video. I also build apps, lately with an AI pair-programmer at my elbow. The one I care about most is PaQueueKa, because I play badminton and I wanted the queue at our courts to run itself.

It turns out it’s all the same hobby. A street at rush hour, a pipeline at 2 a.m. and a court with twelve people waiting are each a flow of things that wants a fair order.

How a photo gets here

This site is a small pipeline. Every photo above rode this line.

  1. Phone Shot on my phone, out in the street.
  2. /admin I upload it with a title and a caption.
  3. Cloudinary Keeps the original and makes every size on request.
  4. Postgres One row in gallery_items: title, caption, order. {{ total_photos }} row{{ 's' if total_photos != 1 }}{% if latest_upload %} · newest {{ latest_upload | stamp }}{% endif %}
  5. Flask Reads the rows, asks Cloudinary for sizes, renders this page.
  6. You HTML from Flask, pixels straight from Cloudinary at the width your screen needs. 5 widths per photo

PaQueueKa

A queue app for badminton and pickleball open play. The host sets up the courts; the app works out who plays next.

Open play used to run on a whiteboard, a clipboard and a lot of “sino next?”. So I built the app I wanted at our courts. Players join by scanning a code, no account needed. Matches are balanced by skill tier. Record a score and the queue rotates itself.

  • Q1Join with a code or QR, no sign-up
  • Q2Fair, skill-balanced matches
  • Q3Next Up plans every court ahead
