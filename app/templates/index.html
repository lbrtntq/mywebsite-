{# ── About ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

I’m Albert, a data engineer from the Philippines. I notice how things move, and I like putting them in order.

By day I move data from where it lands to where it’s useful, and keep it moving. Off the clock I walk around with my phone out: a street at rush hour, a crowd crossing a square, towers stacked against the sky. I vlog a little and make the occasional video.

I also build apps, lately with an AI pair-programmer beside me. The one I care about most is PaQueueKa, because I play badminton and I wanted the queue at our courts to run itself.

Traffic, pipelines and a court with twelve people waiting turn out to be the same puzzle: things in motion that want a fair order.

{# ── PaQueueKa ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

PaQueueKa

A queue app for badminton and pickleball open play. The host sets up the courts; the app works out who plays next.

Open play used to run on a whiteboard and a lot of “sino next?”. Now players join by scanning a code, no account needed. Matches are balanced by skill. Record a score and the queue rotates itself. I designed it, built it, and ship it to both app stores myself.
